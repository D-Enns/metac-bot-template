Between June 13 and June 24, 2025, Israel launched a major air-and-missile campaign (Operation Rising Lion) against Iran’s nuclear and military infrastructure, striking sites at Natanz, Fordo and Isfahan, senior scientists, air-defence installations and leadership targets. Leaked Israeli security-cabinet transcripts reveal that Prime Minister Netanyahu and military chiefs framed the strikes as a historic pre-emptive move to prevent a future Iranian nuclear threat and to establish a “balance of terror.” Iran responded with waves of ballistic missile and drone attacks on Tel Aviv, Haifa, Jerusalem and other cities, causing civilian casualties and significant urban damage that commentators likened to Gaza. Simultaneously, cyber warfare surged: hacktivist groups launched thousands of DDoS attacks and data exfiltration operations against both countries and neighboring states, while Iran imposed near-total internet shutdowns. Ahead of the Jewish high holidays later in 2025, Israeli security agencies warned of heightened Iran-backed terror threats to Israelis abroad. Iranian leaders, meanwhile, have issued defiant rhetoric promising a “new system” arising from regional destruction and have declared readiness to counter any further U.S. or Israeli aggression.
Analysts and commentators diverge on the likelihood of renewed large-scale conflict before year’s end. Several Western and regional experts, including Foreign Policy and German-language outlets, predict that Israel may conduct another pre-emptive strike against Iran between late 2025 and early 2026, while numerology-based forecasts and prophecies also anticipate clashes in that window. Al Jazeera and Middle East Eye pieces emphasize that domestic political pressures in Israel and diplomatic moves—such as threatened EU sanctions and U.S. support—will shape the timing of any new operation. Conversely, some Arab-language analysts argue that Iran’s nuclear program remains resilient and that diplomatic channels with the IAEA and the European “troika” could avert immediate war, suggesting the near-term risk of another full-scale confrontation is low.

'A historic moment': leaked transcripts reveal secret deliberations at start of Iran war
The Times of Israel reports that leaked transcripts from a secret Israeli security‑cabinet meeting on 12 June 2025 reveal the decision‑making behind the 13 June overnight strikes on Iran. Iran-Israel War Triggers a Maelstrom in Cyberspace
On June 13, Israel launched Operation Rising Lion, a missile strike campaign against Iran, which triggered a surge of cyberattacks. Analysts have identified more than 100 distinct threat actors targeting Iran or Israel, with Israel experiencing a higher volume of attacks. From June 4‑12, Radware researchers logged 3‑4 DDoS attacks per day against Israel; the count spiked to 21 on June 13 and 34 on June 14, and has averaged 25 or more daily since. Israel now accounts for nearly 40 % of all global hacktivist DDoS attacks. In contrast, Iran has faced fewer but less sophisticated attacks. Iran’s government declared a cyber DEFCON 1, shutting down internet and phone connectivity for citizens; bandwidth was cut by about 80 % on June 17. The Iranian Cyber Security Command warned that ‘the Zionist regime has launched a massive cyber war against the country's digital infrastructure,’ and that ‘so far, many of these attacks have been successfully repelled.’ The state also directed citizens to use a national intranet and reportedly allowed Starlink terminals, though the service is illegal. Hacktivists such as the pro‑Palestine ‘Handala’ actor leaked 425 GB of data from Israeli shipping company Mor Logistics Ltd. and claimed 4 TB of documents from the Weizmann Institute of Science. A separate Israel‑aligned group, Predatory Sparrow, disrupted Iran’s Bank Sepah website and ATMs on June 13. Analysts note that hacktivist activity also targets neighboring countries, with Egypt, Jordan, and Saudi Arabia among the most frequently DDoSed. The IT‑ISAC and Food and Ag‑ISAC warned U.S. companies to prepare for increased attacks from Iranian‑affiliated actors.
