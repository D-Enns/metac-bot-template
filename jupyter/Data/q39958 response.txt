Spirit Airlines filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection for the second time on August 29–30, 2025, less than six months after emerging from its prior restructuring. The carrier cited weak leisure demand, high operating costs and a debt load exceeding US$1–2 billion as drivers of the new filing, and reported a Q2 2025 net loss of $245.8 million and negative free cash flow of $1 billion. Under the reorganization plan, Spirit will continue normal operations while redesigning its network to focus on key markets, rightsizing its fleet, cutting routes (including service to 11 airports), furloughing around 270 pilots and demoting 140 captains, and addressing its cost structure through measures such as bundled fares and premium seating options (Spirit First, Premium Economy and Value).
In response to Spirit’s financial distress, rival carriers have pursued strategies to capture its market share. United Airlines CEO Scott Kirby repeatedly ruled out acquiring Spirit’s assets—citing $15 million in plane-modification costs per aircraft and insufficient gate slots—and has added routes in former Spirit markets, a tactic mirrored by Frontier Airlines. Kirby forecasted the end of the ultra-low-cost model, arguing that discount carriers’ networks contain double-digit percentages of unprofitable routes. Meanwhile, Spirit ran a US$54.99 one-way fare promotion for Hispanic Heritage Month to retain customers and reinforce its value positioning amid restructuring.
