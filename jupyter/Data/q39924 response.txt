Spirit Airlines filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection for the second time on August 29–30, 2025, less than six months after emerging from its prior restructuring in March 2025. CEO Dave Davis announced a comprehensive reorganisation focused on network optimisation—cutting overall capacity by about 25% for its November 2025 schedule, removing service to at least 12 cities, and negotiating with vendors and aircraft lessors to shrink the business. The restructuring includes furloughing roughly 270 pilots, demoting about 140 captains, and offering voluntary unpaid leaves to flight attendants. Since exiting Chapter 11 in March, Spirit has reported losses of approximately $245–257 million through mid-2025, carries about $2.4 billion in long-term debt, and has warned of “substantial doubt” about its ability to continue as a going concern without fresh funding.
The carrier’s financial distress has prompted rivals such as Frontier and JetBlue to expand into former Spirit markets, while United’s CEO publicly predicted Spirit’s collapse and declared the low-cost model unsustainable. An incident in mid-September 2025, when a Spirit flight drifted dangerously close to Air Force One, underscored ongoing operational strains. Despite efforts to rebrand with bundled fares and new premium options, Spirit’s stock has tumbled—down more than 40% on one trading day—and its liquidity crunch has led it to engage advisers for strategic options.
