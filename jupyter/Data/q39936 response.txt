Real estate redux: Why 2025's housing market feels like 2024
The Denver Metro Association of Realtors reports that, despite a 22% rise in active listings to 13,059 in August 2025, closed sales have stayed at 2024 levels, with 3,636 transactions – a 6% drop from July’s 3,861 and also 6% lower than August 2024’s 3,850.  The median days on market climbed 25% from July’s 24 days to 30 days, and 43% above the August 2024 average of 21 days.  Sales volume fell 3% to $2.6 billion from July’s $2.7 billion, and the median close price edged up 1% to $593,250 from July’s $588,500.  Amanda Snitker, chair of the market trends committee, said, 'As we enter the fall months, there is little expectation for the market to change as we round out the year,' and added, 'If you cannot understand the trends or refuse to see the story they reveal, charts and stats are meaningless.'  She also noted a widening gap between quickly selling homes and those lingering: only 1% of closed properties had price reductions, whereas 58% of active listings had been reduced, with a median drop of 4.5%.  Andrew Abrams highlighted that high‑end homes over $1 million continue to sell, noting that 'Essentially, properties are being listed higher, negotiated down, but still selling for more on average than in the past two years.'  The report underscores that strategic pricing remains crucial in a market where days on market are rising but prices remain largely stable.
