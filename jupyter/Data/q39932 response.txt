Trump revives fight to oust Fed Governor ahead of looming rate cut
Donald Trump has renewed his effort to remove Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook just days before the Fed is expected to announce its first interest‑rate hike in almost a year. The DOJ filed an appeal on September 14 in the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit after a lower‑court ruling temporarily blocked Trump from firing Cook. Trump’s lawyers argue that the President may freely oust Fed officials ‘for cause’ and that courts cannot review the decision. Trump accuses Cook of ‘deceitful and potentially criminal conduct,’ claiming she misrepresented details about one of her mortgage properties and announced his decision to fire her in August 2025. Cook challenged her dismissal in court, contending that the President’s move violated her right to due process. Judge Jia Cobb issued a preliminary injunction on September 9, blocking Trump’s order and allowing Cook to retain her position. The DOJ filed a detailed brief in the appellate court, arguing that the President can fire members of the Federal Reserve Board ‘for cause.’ NBC News reported that a 2021 loan summary described Cook’s Atlanta property as a second home, not a primary residence, undermining the accusation that she misrepresented her property on mortgage applications. If this evidence holds up, it could dismantle the prosecution’s case and raise questions about whether Trump’s attempt to fire Cook was about alleged misconduct or exerting political influence over the Fed. Meanwhile, the Fed is expected to lower rates on Wednesday, possibly by a quarter of a percentage point to a benchmark range of 4.0–4.25 %. Chief economist Joe Brusuelas warned that while traders can expect one cut in September, three separate cuts before year‑end are almost impossible because the Fed’s data does not show enough weakness. The Fed’s leadership is also in focus, as Chair Jerome Powell’s term ends soon and potential successors such as BlackRock executive Rick Rieder emerge. The outcome of Cook’s case could influence future governors’ willingness to align with the White House.

U.S. Federal Reserve Prepares for First Rate Cut in 2025 Amid Rising Political Tensions
The Federal Reserve is widely expected to make its first interest‑rate cut this year, with analysts forecasting a 25‑basis‑point reduction at the upcoming policy meeting, as the labor market weakens.  The Fed has kept rates in the 4.25‑4.50% band since its last cut in December, while monitoring the impact of President Trump’s broad tariff‑related fiscal pressures on inflation.
Joash Lipski, head of the International Economics Department at the Atlantic Council, said, 'It is interesting to see what the Fed will do at its meeting, and it is clear.'  He added, 'There is a lot of tension surrounding this meeting.'  The article notes that Trump has withdrawn threats to fire Fed Chair Jerome Powell over the cost of relocating the Fed’s Washington headquarters, but he did attempt to remove Governor Lisa Cook in August on allegations of mortgage fraud.  Cook, the first Black woman to serve on the Fed’s Board, sued her dismissal and remains in office pending litigation.
The early resignation of another governor, Adriana Kugler, left a vacancy that Trump’s chief economic adviser, Steven Miran, could fill if confirmed by a Republican‑majority Senate.  Miran, who heads the White House Economic Advisers Council, faces criticism from Democrats over his proposed vacation plans.
Markets will focus on indicators of the pace and size of future Fed cuts.  KPMG’s Diane Sounk predicts the Fed will signal the start of a ‘dovish cycle’ it is reluctant to commit to.  The Consumer Price Index rose to 2.9% in August, the highest pace since the beginning of the year.  Wells Fargo noted that the labor market is in a precarious position, with sluggish job growth, declining worker morale, and unemployment exceeding many full‑employment estimates.
Lipski warned that the Fed’s policy board, which typically votes by near‑unanimous consensus, may face a split over whether to cut rates by 25 basis points, 50 basis points, or hold steady.  He said, 'This is not something we have seen from the Fed before.  It is a group that has voted almost unanimously for decades.'  He cautioned that broader changes in Fed composition could happen faster than markets anticipate, noting that the 12 regional Fed presidents are reappointed every five years, a process that could lead to a full board turnover.
Overall, the article reports on the Fed’s anticipated rate cut, the political pressures surrounding its leadership, and the market’s focus on inflation and employment data, while citing expert commentary and recent political developments.

US Fed poised for first rate cut of 2025 as political tension mounts
The U.S. Federal Reserve is expected to cut interest rates by 25 basis points at its two‑day policy meeting on Wednesday, following a month‑long push from President Donald Trump and a weakening jobs market.  Since its last reduction in December, the Fed has kept rates in the 4.25%‑4.50% range while monitoring the impact of Trump’s tariffs on inflation.  Analysts predict the cut, with markets watching the Fed’s future pace and size of rate reductions.  "What's interesting is that it's very clear what the Fed is going to do when they meet," said Josh Lipsky, chair of international economics at the Atlantic Council, adding that "there's high drama around this meeting" due to personnel issues on the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC).  Trump’s political pressure is evident: he has dropped threats to oust Chair Jerome Powell, fired Governor Lisa Cook in August over mortgage‑fraud allegations, and is moving to appoint his chief economic adviser Stephen Miran to the Fed board, a nomination that has drawn criticism from Democratic lawmakers.  Cook, the first Black woman on the Fed’s board, has mounted a legal challenge to her removal and remains in place while the lawsuit proceeds.  The early resignation of Governor Adriana Kugler created a vacancy that Trump has rushed to fill.  Market participants will also focus on Powell’s remarks about inflation risks, as the consumer price index rose to 2.9% in August—its highest pace since the start of the year—and the labor market shows stagnant growth, deteriorating worker sentiment, and an unemployment rate above many full‑employment estimates.  "The inflation genie has not quite been put back into the bottle," noted Wells Fargo analysts.  With Miran’s potential arrival, analysts warn that the FOMC could face unprecedented division over whether to cut 25 basis points, 50 basis points, or keep rates unchanged, a scenario "not something we're used to seeing from the Fed," Lipsky said.  The article concludes that broader changes to the Fed’s composition could happen more swiftly than markets expect, as presidents of the 12 regional Fed banks are up for reappointment every five years, potentially allowing the board of governors to replace them—an event that has not occurred before.

Trump administration asks appeals court to let president fire Fed governor Lisa Cook before key meeting
On September 12, 2025, the Trump administration asked the U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit to stay a ruling that blocked the president’s attempt to remove Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook. The Justice Department argued that the president has broad discretion to remove federal officials and that courts have only narrow authority to review such decisions. The government cited evidence that Cook applied for two personal loans and obtained favorable interest rates by misrepresenting her residence, stating that 'Here, the evidence—which Cook has yet to offer contrary explanation for—was that she applied for two loans for her personal benefit, and was able to obtain favorable interest rates by misrepresenting where she lived,' and that this misconduct 'indisputably calls into question Cook's trustworthiness and whether she can be a responsible steward of the interest rates and economy for the whole Nation.' The request also urged the court to act by the end of the day on Monday because the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) would meet on Tuesday and Wednesday, and an administrative stay could 'threaten Governor Cook's participation in next week's meeting and potentially plunge the FOMC's vote into turmoil,' potentially impacting domestic and foreign markets. The FOMC, which includes Cook, Chair Jerome Powell, and 10 other top central bank officials, sets the Fed's interest rate targets that influence economic growth, inflation, and borrowing costs. Powell’s term ends in May, while Cook’s term on the Fed’s seven‑member Board of Governors ends in 2038. Trump moved to fire Cook in late August, and the allegations are not related to his push against the Fed; however, removing her would allow the president to appoint a replacement and increase his influence over the central bank.
