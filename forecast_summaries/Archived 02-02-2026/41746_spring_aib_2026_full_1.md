Eli Lilly (LLY) reported a 45% quarterly revenue increase in Q4 2024, driven primarily by strong sales of Mounjaro and Zepbound, with new product revenue more than doubling to $5.6 billion—$3.5 billion from global Mounjaro sales and $1.9 billion from U.S. Zepbound sales. R&D expenses rose 18% as the company initiated eight new Phase III programs in 2024. SG&A costs increased 26%, largely due to U.S. and international launch activities, including direct-to-consumer advertising for Zepbound and Mounjaro. U.S. revenue grew 40%, with 45% volume growth, though realized prices declined 5% due to rebate and discount changes. Europe revenue grew 82% in constant currency (61% excluding a one-time alliance payment), Japan rose 27%, and China increased 13%. Zepbound became the leader in new U.S. anti-obesity prescriptions. Verzenio U.S. prescriptions grew 15%, while worldwide Trulicity revenue declined 25% due to patient switches to Mounjaro and lower prices. Eli Lilly met its production target, delivering 1.5x the incretin salable doses in H2 2024 versus H2 2023. For 2025, the company forecasted revenue of $58 billion to $61 billion (midpoint: 32% growth), non-GAAP EPS of $22.50 to $24.00, and a portfolio-level margin outlook of 41.5% to 43.5% (340 basis points expansion). The company returned $3 billion to shareholders via dividends and buybacks, approved a new $15 billion share repurchase program, and raised its quarterly dividend by 15% for the seventh consecutive year.
