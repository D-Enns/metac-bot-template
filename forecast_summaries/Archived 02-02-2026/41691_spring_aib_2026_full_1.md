**2026 Costa Rican Elections: How Results Will Be Transmitted—TSE Explains**
The Supreme Electoral Tribunal (TSE) of Costa Rica announced the procedures for transmitting election results for the 2026 elections, scheduled for February 1, 2026. A total of 3,731,788 Costa Ricans—67,270 abroad—will vote for president, vice-presidents, and deputies. Preliminary results will be released at 8:45 p.m. on February 1, covering data from 7,154 polling stations (JRV) across 2,103 voting centers, including 49 consulates in 42 countries. Transmission will occur via two systems: mobile devices used in 62% of JRVs (4,422 stations), with 450 TSE officials transmitting data through a private app developed by the TSE; and a call center network with 150 operators across three centers—TSE, Radiográfica Costarricense (Racsa), and Instituto Costarricense de Electricidad (ICE). Results from overseas centers, operating from 9 a.m. to 7 p.m. local time, will be transmitted via phone to the TSE, with a processing window from 6 a.m. to 12 p.m. on February 2 (local time). Preliminary results will be updated every 15 minutes until 10 p.m., then as data flows. Citizens can access results via the TSE website (www.tse.go.cr), the free mobile app #VotanteInformadoCR, and the TSE’s social media platforms (Facebook, X, YouTube). Cybersecurity measures include strict verification protocols: only authorized personnel with valid codes can transmit data, and a final scrutiny by the TSE ensures accuracy, with discrepancies under 0.5%. President Eugenia Zamora Chavarría emphasized that the collaboration between state-owned entities ICE and Racsa ensures no third-party involvement or privatization, guaranteeing transparency and security. The system acts as a two-tier filter: initial data validation and final official declaration.
