**Resolution of January 15, 2026, by the Treasury and Financial Policy Directorate General: Call for Treasury Liquidity Auction with Daily Maturity**
On January 15, 2026, the Dirección General del Tesoro y Política Financiera issued a resolution calling for a liquidity auction to conduct double purchase-sale operations with daily maturity, valid for 42 days. These operations are authorized under Orden ECE/417/2019, which permits active treasury management operations to temporarily generate returns on state treasury resources. The auction and resulting operations are governed by the Resolution of December 15, 2023, which establishes general conditions for temporary asset acquisition and time deposit operations. The interest rate to be paid is variable, calculated as the sum of the differential offered by the winning entity—either positive or negative—and the euro short-term reference rate (€STR). Each participating entity must submit a bid with a rate at least 2% of the total auctioned amount. In the event of a second round, only entities that participated in and were awarded in the first round may participate, and the 2% minimum bid requirement from the first round does not apply. The resolution was signed by the Director General of Treasury and Financial Policy, with the signature delegated by Resolution of February 29, 2024, and countersigned by the Subdirector General of State Treasury Management, Manuel Varela Bellido.

**Income Tax Refund Stuck? Types of Notices Issued by the Department and How to Reply | LatestLY**
Income tax refunds for the 2024-25 financial year (Assessment Year 2025-26) are experiencing delays, particularly for refunds exceeding INR 30,000, due to stricter verification, data mismatches, and enhanced risk-based scrutiny by the Income Tax Department. The department is using automated data analytics to cross-check income declared in returns with Form 26AS and the Annual Information Statement (AIS), flagging even minor discrepancies in bank interest, dividend income, or capital gains. Returns claiming higher deductions not previously disclosed via Form 12BB are also subject to manual review to prevent false claims. Taxpayers may receive one of four notices: a tax demand notice (requiring payment or rectification), a defective return notice (with 15 days to correct errors), a refund adjustment notice (adjusting the refund against a prior-year tax demand), or a scrutiny notice (requiring submission of supporting documents). To resolve delays, taxpayers should check the refund or demand status on the income tax e-filing portal, revalidate bank details (including IFSC code), and submit a refund reissue request if needed. Nudge alerts via email or SMS can help identify mismatches and allow voluntary revised returns. The Centralised Processing Centre has until December 31, 2026, to process returns; if delays are not taxpayer-caused, the department must pay interest at 0.5% per month (6% annually). To prevent issues, taxpayers should ensure their PAN is linked with Aadhaar and their bank account is pre-validated on the portal.
