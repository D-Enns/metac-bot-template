Unlike SpaceX's Starship or Blue Origin's Zhuque-3, which use vertical landing, the 'Canglong-1' engine employs ADHL technology—using aerodynamic shape to generate lift for deceleration and horizontal glide landing, similar to a spaceplane but without wings. This method results in minimal payload loss (only 1%) compared to vertical recovery (around 30%), eliminates the need for complex components like grid fins, landing legs, or retro-thrusters, reduces structural complexity and cost, improves landing accuracy, and allows landing on standard airport runways. On May 21, 2025, the ADHL scheme became the first such commercial launch vehicle design in China to pass authoritative review, establishing a third recovery pathway for Chinese commercial spaceflight—alongside vertical and parachute recovery.
